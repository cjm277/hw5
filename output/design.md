# Campus Customs: The Agent Desk (Problem 8 design)

The dashboard is the shop's back office: a desk where a manager watches five agents work a ticket, then signs off on any money. It's built in `frontend/` (React + Vite + TypeScript), runs at `http://localhost:5173`, and calls the Problem 7 FastAPI routes at `http://localhost:8000`.

**Where the look comes from.** The look is borrowed from my Homework 4 storefront so the back office feels like the same business as the shop floor. I kept:

- the Yale palette (navy `#00356b`, royal `#2456d3`, powder `#b9d4f1`, white, warm off-white `#f7f5ef`);
- Inter for all text and Saira heavy italic for the Campus Customs "EST. 1975" wordmark;
- the navy backdrop tiled with white serif **Y**s, with the page sitting on it like a framed panel.

**What I added and dropped.** The photos are gone because they would distract from the work. Three colours carry meaning on top of the palette: **green** means success (resolved, approve), **yellow** means the team is done but a human still has to act, and **red** means stop (reject, failed).

## Layout

| Choice | What it looks like | Why it feels unique / why people enjoy it |
|---|---|---|
| **A framed desk on the Y backdrop** | The navy Y-pattern backdrop from the storefront fills the window. The board sits on top as one off-white panel with rounded corners and a deep shadow. | It's instantly "Campus Customs" and not a generic admin template. The frame makes the board feel like an object you sit down at, not an endless web page. |
| **Navy header with the wordmark** | The storefront hero's navy gradient, Y pattern, and light wordmark, titled "The Agent Desk". The tagline is "Five agents, one ticket at a time. Nothing gets paid until you sign." A small pill says whether the backend is connected and names the team's model. | The tagline states the house rule up front. The connection pill answers the first question anyone asks ("is it on?") before they click. |
| **Three columns, in the order you work** | **Left:** the ticket tray (pick a ticket). **Centre:** the floor where the agents sit, plus the run summary underneath. **Right:** the live desk log. | Your eye moves left to right the way the job flows: pick a ticket, watch the team, read what they said. |
| **Your signature sits with the team** | "Needs your signature" is part of the agent floor, in its top-right corner, level with the Boss. When nothing is waiting it's a calm white card. When an agent prepares a payment or order, it turns yellow, glows with a slow pulse, and reads "✍ Your turn" with a count. | The human is part of the team, sitting next to the Boss, not in a sidebar. You notice the moment it's your turn without hunting for it. |
| **"On the desk" card next to the Boss** | The selected ticket sits beside the Boss with the one big button: **Send to the agent team**. | The main action lives where the action happens, not in a toolbar. You hand the ticket to the Boss, which is how it works in a real shop. |
| **Small extras, kept modest** | A run timer ("Working on #103 · 0:46"), a "Latest hand-off" ribbon between the Boss's row and the team's row, toasts for results, a "Signing as" name for approvals, and a **Reset shop data** button (with confirm) to restore the original database. | Each extra answers a question a manager would actually have: how long has it been, who has it now, did my click work, can I start over. |
| **Responsive and calm** | On narrower screens the approvals and log move under the floor, and then everything stacks. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off every animation. | It still works on a laptop beside a register, and it respects people who find motion tiring. |

## How the agents read differently

| Choice | What it looks like | Why it feels unique / why people enjoy it |
|---|---|---|
| **Five little people at five desks** | Each agent is a hand-drawn SVG person sitting at a walnut desk in a navy office chair. Each desk has a brass-edged navy nameplate showing the role and its status (At desk / Working / Done). The Boss sits on top, centred; the four specialists sit in a row below a dashed line, like an org chart you can see. | People read faces and outfits faster than labels. You can tell who's busy at a glance across the room. |
| **Outfits that match the job** | **Boss:** navy suit, white blouse, Yale-blue pin, coffee mug with "CC". **Inventory:** hi-vis orange vest with reflective stripes, yellow hard hat, a cardboard box marked "M" in both hands. **Accounting:** green eyeshade visor, glasses, powder argyle sweater vest, navy bow tie, a calculator showing 840.00, a receipt. **Facilities:** blue work coveralls with a "FAC" name patch, a Campus Customs cap, a wrench in hand, a red toolbox. **Customer Service:** headset with mic, powder cardigan, name tag, an envelope, and a laptop with the "C" logo. | Each role is obvious from the clothes and props alone, and the details reward a second look. It gives the team personality, so the board is fun to watch instead of a wall of logs. |
| **A team that looks like a real campus, not a stereotype** | The job shows in the outfit, never in the person. Skin tones and genders are mixed on purpose so none follows the usual job cliché: the Boss is a Black woman with natural hair, Inventory is a light-skinned woman with a ponytail under the hard hat, Accounting is a brown-skinned man, Facilities is a woman with a bob under her cap, and Customer Service is a man on the headset. | Anyone sitting at this desk can picture themselves in any role. It feels like a real, modern campus shop and avoids quietly teaching "who does which job". |
| **Each one works in their own way** | When idle, everyone breathes and blinks at a different moment, and the Boss's coffee steams. When working, each does their own action. The **Boss** gestures and nods, **Inventory** lifts the box, **Accounting** taps the calculator while the screen flickers, **Facilities** turns the wrench, and **Customer Service** talks into the headset with sound waves. Everyone's mouth moves while they "speak". When done, the agent does a little hop and gets a green check badge. | Motion shows *what kind* of work is happening, not just *that* something is happening. A quick look tells you "Accounting is crunching numbers", which matches the speech bubble. |
| **Speech bubbles in plain English** | The bubble over each agent shows the latest thing they said or did. Tool calls are rewritten as sentences: `check_stock` becomes "Counting CC-HOOD-NAVY size M on the shelf (need 20)." Hand-offs read "Handing this to Inventory: …". Working bubbles have a royal border; done bubbles have a green one. Thinking shows as three bouncing dots. | It reads like overhearing a team, not like reading a stack trace. Managers can follow it without knowing tool names, and the raw tool name still sits under the desk for anyone auditing. |
| **Who asked whom** | Under each desk: the live tool chip, plus "Asked by Boss, Customer Service" when teammates hand off work. A count like "Working ×2" appears when two teammates ask the same agent at once. The "Latest hand-off" ribbon shows "Accounting → Inventory" and the task. | Delegation is the interesting part of a multi-agent team, and this makes it visible instead of hidden in a log. |
| **A colour per agent in the log** | The desk log is a chat-style feed. Each agent has an avatar colour: navy Boss, orange Inventory, green Accounting, slate Facilities, royal Customer Service. Hand-offs are powder bubbles with "→ Inventory"; final answers are green bubbles. Tool chips sit under each step. | You can scan the log by colour to follow one agent's thread through a busy run. |

## How resolved tickets show up

| Choice | What it looks like | Why it feels unique / why people enjoy it |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow until you've done your part, then green** | When a run finishes and the team left a payment or order for you, the ticket turns **yellow**: a yellow chip "✍ Team done, waiting on human", a yellow edge and wash, and "Team done, waiting on you: 1 request needs your signature →" on the desk. Once you approve or reject everything for that ticket, or if the team needed nothing from you, it turns **green**: "✓ Resolved", a green edge and wash, and "Resolved. Nothing is left for a human to do." A toast says when your signature was the last thing it needed. While running, the chip is royal with a pulsing dot ("Team working"); a failed run is red. | Green only ever means truly done, so it's a promise you can trust. Yellow tells you exactly whose turn it is, and the arrow points to where you act. |
| **A rubber stamp on the summary** | "What the team did" opens with a tilted stamp that thumps into place: yellow **WAITING ON YOU** (with a note pointing to "Needs your signature") while you still have a decision, green **✓ RESOLVED** once you don't. | It's a physical, satisfying "done" moment, like stamping a paper ticket at a real desk, and the stamp changes the moment you sign. |
| **A short summary of each agent** | First, the Boss's call in a navy-edged box: the decision, what "For you" means, the final status, hand-off and step counts, and anything still blocking. Then one card per agent that worked: a mini portrait, how many tasks, its final answer, its board actions, and the tools it used ("check_stock ×3"). | It answers "what happened?" in about ten seconds, while every claim still traces to a tool. |
| **The customer draft as a real letter** | Customer Service's draft appears on cream paper in a serif font, tilted slightly, labelled "Draft reply · waiting on the board, not sent". | Reading the actual message the way the customer would is the best check on tone. The label makes it clear nothing has gone out. |
| **What counts as "a human task"** | A human task is a payment or purchase order the team prepared for that ticket that is still pending, because that's what the board lets a human finish. Customer drafts stay on the board and are never sent, so they don't hold a ticket in yellow. The tray card also shows the Boss's own status in the database ("Desk status: …") so nothing is hidden. Once you've made the last decision, the backend also marks the ticket `resolved` in the database, so the board and the database agree. | The colour follows something you can actually do on this page, so a ticket never gets stuck in yellow waiting for a button that doesn't exist. |

## How cash shows up

| Choice | What it looks like | Why it feels unique / why people enjoy it |
|---|---|---|
| **Top right, big, always visible** | A white card in the header's top-right corner with a powder border, labelled "Checking balance", in heavy navy numerals with a royal "$". Underneath: "as of" the shop date, and, once orders are placed, how much is committed to orders and how much is free. | Cash is the one number that limits every decision, so it gets the most prominent spot. |
| **Slot-machine reels** | Every digit sits in its own little reel. When the balance changes, the reels spin through the numbers and settle left to right like a slot machine. The balance also spins up from zero when the page loads. | It turns a plain number change into a moment you notice, so you can't miss that money moved. |
| **It only moves when a human signs** | Approving a payment calls the backend's approve route, which is the only path that lowers cash. The balance refreshes at once and the reels spin down. A red "−$840.00" chip floats under the balance and fades; a reset shows a green "+" chip. A toast confirms, for example "Approved by … and paid. Cash is now $2,560.00." | Cause and effect are obvious: you sign, the money visibly leaves. That reinforces the rule that only humans move money. |
| **Approval cards show the cash effect first** | At the top right of the floor, each pending item shows its title (for example "Pay invoice 501" or "Purchase order · 12 × CC-HOOD-NAVY M"), the amount in large type, who prepared it, and why. Below that, a green note says what happens if you approve ("Pays it now. Checking drops to $2,560.00") or an orange warning says the desk will refuse it because cash can't cover it. **Approve is green; Reject is red.** Reject asks for a reason, which the team sees. Any refusal from the backend shows right on the card. | You see the consequence before you sign, like a good accountant handing you a cheque with a note clipped to it. The red/green buttons follow the convention everyone already knows. |

## Notes

- **Code:**
  - `frontend/src/index.css` (palette, Y pattern, figure animations, all styles)
  - `components/AgentFigure.tsx` (the five people)
  - `components/SlotBalance.tsx` (reels)
  - `AgentStation`, `TicketTray`, `ApprovalsPanel`, `DeskLog`, `RunSummary`
  - `floor.ts`, which turns `/events` into who's working, what they said, and per-agent summaries
  - `api.ts` (every backend call; `API_BASE = http://localhost:8000`)
- **Backend change for the browser:** `backend/main.py` allows the Vite origin with CORS (`http://localhost:5173` and `http://127.0.0.1:5173`, GET/POST). Vite is pinned to port 5173 (`strictPort`) so the origin always matches.
- **How the board stays live:** every 4 s it polls tickets, cash, approvals, and health. While a run is going, it polls `/runs/{id}` and `/events?run_id=…&after=…` every second. A finished run is remembered in the browser, so reloading keeps the green "Resolved" and the summary. Reset clears that memory.
- **Accessibility:** good contrast on off-white, a visible royal focus outline, ARIA labels on the figures and the balance, and all motion off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Fonts** load from Google Fonts like the storefront. There are no images: every figure and prop is inline SVG, so the board loads instantly and stays sharp at any size.
